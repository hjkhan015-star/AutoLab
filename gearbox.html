Gearbox. The input shaft drives the countershaft through a single gear pair. Fixed gears on the countershaft always spin at the same rate. Each output gear is free on the output shaft until its synchro hub locks it — the selected gear then forces the output shaft to turn at that gear's ratio. Reverse uses an extra idler gear to flip the output direction.
Watch the hub, not just the colour: only the engaged gear's synchro hub slides over and the gear turns fully solid with a glowing ring. Every other gear stays dim and semi-transparent — it's still spinning, but it isn't locked to the output shaft, so it isn't transmitting any power.
Typical passenger car ratios — 1st 3.50:1 · 2nd 2.00:1 · 3rd 1.40:1 · 4th 1.00:1 (direct) · R 3.60:1
Or use the gear stick (bottom-right) — drag the knob through the H-pattern grooves to select a gear.
