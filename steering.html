Rack-and-pinion steering. Turning the steering wheel rotates the steering column, which turns the pinion gear. The pinion teeth mesh with the rack — a flat bar with teeth cut along it — so every turn of the pinion slides the rack sideways by rack travel = pinion radius × pinion angle. Tie rods bolted to each end of the rack push or pull the steering arms on the front wheels, which pivot about their kingpins — so both road wheels steer in unison. Drag the wheel on the right to steer; release it and it self-centres.
