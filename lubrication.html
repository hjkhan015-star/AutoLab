Wet-sump forced-feed lubrication. Oil collects in the sump at the bottom of the engine. A gear-type oil pump, driven directly off the crankshaft, draws oil up through a pickup tube fitted with a strainer that screens out debris. The pump forces oil through the oil filter, which traps contaminants — if the filter is cold or clogged, a spring-loaded bypass valve lets oil flow around it rather than starve the engine. Filtered oil enters the main oil gallery, a drilled passage running the length of the block, which branches to feed the main bearings supporting the crankshaft, the connecting-rod bearings, and the camshaft. A pressure-relief valve bleeds off excess pressure at high rpm. Oil thrown from the rotating crank splashes onto the cylinder walls and piston skirts, then drains back to the sump by gravity — completing the loop.
