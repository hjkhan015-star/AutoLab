Ignition. The ignition coil is a transformer — 12 V from the battery flows through its primary winding, building a magnetic field. When the ECU breaks that circuit, the field collapses and induces 20,000–40,000 V in the secondary winding. That high voltage jumps the spark plug gap, igniting the air–fuel mixture.

Timing matters. The mixture doesn't burn instantly — it takes a few milliseconds to fully ignite. So the spark must fire before the piston reaches TDC. At idle, this is ~10° before TDC. As RPM rises, that lead must grow, or peak pressure arrives too late and power is lost. This is the timing advance curve.
Modern systems use coil-on-plug — one coil per cylinder, no distributor.
